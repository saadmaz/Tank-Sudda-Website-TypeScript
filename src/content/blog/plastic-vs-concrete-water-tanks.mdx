Most homes and buildings in Sri Lanka store water in either a moulded plastic tank or a built concrete tank (often an underground sump). Both need regular cleaning, but they age differently, so the things worth checking are not quite the same.

## Plastic tanks

Moulded plastic tanks are common on rooftops and stands. They are light, quick to install and have smooth internal walls.

### What to check

- **The lid.** Plastic lids can crack, warp in the sun or lose their fit. A loose lid is the most common way dust, leaves and insects get in.
- **Sun exposure.** Over years in direct sun, plastic can become brittle. Look for cracks around the inlet, outlet and lid.
- **The stand.** Make sure the base is level and solid. A full 1,000 L tank weighs around a tonne, so a weak stand is a safety risk.
- **Fittings.** Check that the inlet and outlet connections are not leaking or cracked.

### Cleaning

Smooth plastic walls do not hold deposits as easily as rough surfaces, but sediment still settles on the floor and a film can form on the walls. Plastic should be cleaned with methods that will not scratch or damage the surface, because scratches give deposits more to cling to.

## Concrete tanks and sumps

Concrete is used for larger tanks and for most underground sumps. It is strong and long-lasting, but its surface is rougher than plastic.

### What to check

- **Cracks and seepage.** Look for cracks in walls you can see, damp patches around the outside, or water levels that drop when no one is using water.
- **The cover.** Underground sumps are especially exposed to surface water and debris if the cover is damaged, poorly fitting or not raised above the surrounding ground.
- **Surface condition.** Over time the internal surface can wear and become rougher, which makes it easier for deposits to build up.

### Cleaning

Rough concrete surfaces tend to hold more deposits than plastic, so a thorough [deep clean](/services/water-tank-deep-cleaning) with surface scrubbing is usually needed rather than sediment removal alone. Large concrete tanks in apartment buildings and other organisations usually need planning around the people who depend on them; see our [commercial and institutional service](/services/commercial-tank-cleaning).

## Which is easier to look after?

Neither is maintenance-free. A plastic tank is usually easier to inspect and clean; a concrete tank lasts longer but needs closer attention to cracks and covers. Whichever you have, the basics are the same:

1. Keep the lid or cover secure and in good condition.
2. Inspect from outside regularly (see our [maintenance checklist](/blog/water-tank-maintenance-checklist)).
3. Clean on a regular schedule, and include the sump if water passes through one.

> **Safety reminder:** Never enter a tank or sump to inspect or clean it yourself. Look from outside, and leave work inside to people who are properly equipped.
