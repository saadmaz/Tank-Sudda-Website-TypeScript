Many Sri Lankan homes and buildings store water in an underground sump and pump it up to an overhead tank. Because the sump is below ground and covered, it often goes years without anyone looking inside. These are the signs that it needs attention.

## You have cleaned the overhead tank, but problems come back

If the overhead tank was cleaned recently but cloudy water, grit or sediment returns quickly, the sump is a likely source. Sediment in the sump is pumped straight back up into the clean tank.

## Grit or particles at the taps

Fine sand or particles in taps, shower heads or filters can come from sediment being stirred up and carried through the system. It is worth checking both the sump and the overhead tank.

## The pump is working harder

If the pump runs longer than it used to, cycles more often, or sounds strained, sediment and debris near the pump intake may be part of the problem. A pump technician can check the pump itself; a clean removes the material around it.

## The cover is damaged or poorly fitting

A sump cover that is cracked, broken, loose or level with the surrounding ground lets in dust, leaves, insects and surface water during rain. If the cover has been in poor condition for a while, the sump is likely to need cleaning, and the cover should be repaired.

## Surface water flows near the sump

If rainwater runs or pools over the sump area, it can carry dirt in around a poorly sealed cover. After heavy rain, check whether water collects near the cover.

## It has simply been a long time

If you cannot remember when the sump was last cleaned, or have just moved into a property, it is sensible to have it cleaned and inspected so you know its condition.

## What to do next

1. Do not enter the sump. It is an enclosed space with limited air and access.
2. From outside, note the condition of the cover and anything unusual about your water.
3. Plan to clean the sump and the overhead tank together, so clean water is not stored in a dirty system.
