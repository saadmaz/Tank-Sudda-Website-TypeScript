Heavy monsoon rain and flooding can affect water storage, especially underground sumps. This guide explains what to check and how to plan a clean. It is general guidance, not a substitute for advice from the authorities.

## Put safety first

If floodwater may have entered your sump or tank, **do not drink the stored water** until you have advice from your water supplier or local public health authorities. Follow any official notices for your area.

Electrical equipment such as pumps should only be handled once it is safe to do so. If a pump or its wiring has been under water, have it checked by a qualified electrician before use.

## How floodwater gets in

- **Through the sump cover**, if the cover is level with the ground, damaged or not sealed.
- **Through cracks** in the sump walls when the surrounding ground is saturated.
- **Through overflow or vent openings** that sit low enough for water to reach them.

Overhead tanks are less likely to be reached directly, but they are filled from the sump. If the sump is affected, the overhead tank may be too.

## What to check once the water has gone down

1. Look at the sump cover and the area around it for mud, debris or signs that water stood over it.
2. Note whether your tap water looks cloudy, discoloured or smells unusual.
3. Check for new cracks or damage around the sump and tank.
4. Write down what you see. It helps when you arrange a clean.

Do not enter the sump to look. It is an enclosed space, and after flooding it may contain mud, debris and poor air.

## Plan a clean of the whole system

After flooding, it is sensible to clean both the [sump](/services/sump-cleaning) and the [overhead tank](/services/water-tank-deep-cleaning), so that sediment and debris are removed from the whole storage system. ## Reduce the risk next time

- Raise the sump cover above the surrounding ground where possible.
- Repair cracks and replace damaged covers.
- Make sure rainwater drains away from the sump area.
- Keep overflow and vent openings screened and above likely flood levels.

> **Important:** This article gives general guidance only. Always follow the advice of your water supplier and public health authorities about whether water is safe to use.
